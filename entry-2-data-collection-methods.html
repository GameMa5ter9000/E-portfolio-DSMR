Designing the First Two Data Collection Methods

Based on the reading on tests, questionnaires, and interviews, this entry explains the sample for the study and presents the two data collection methods designed so far: a vocabulary test and a short perception questionnaire.

← Entry #1: Front page Entry #3: Narrative studies & field notes →
01 · sample

Sample of the Project

The study would draw its sample from one third-grade class (students around eight and nine years old) at a Colombian public primary school. Rather than randomly assigning individual students, the intact class would be split into two naturally-occurring groups — a common, non-probability sampling choice in classroom-based research, since schools rarely allow researchers to reshuffle students across sections.

Group A — Matching game

Practises classroom-object vocabulary through the picture–word matching game during the intervention sessions.

Group B — Workbook practice

Continues with the regular workbook-based vocabulary activities the teacher already uses.

02 · why these two methods

Two Methods, Two Different Jobs

A vocabulary test and a short questionnaire were chosen because the study needs two different kinds of evidence. The test produces comparable numbers — immediate and two-week scores for both groups — which is exactly what the research question asks for. The questionnaire, on the other hand, captures something the test cannot: how practical and easy to use the matching game actually felt to the people who experienced it, which speaks directly to one of the study's specific objectives.

In practice, the test would be applied three times (before the intervention, right after it, and two weeks later) to both groups under the same conditions, so scores can be fairly compared. The questionnaire would be applied once, at the end of the intervention, to the teacher and to the students in Group A, to gather quick, low-effort feedback without adding extra class time.

03 · instrument 1

Classroom-Object Vocabulary Test

10 items · picture-to-word matching format · same version used pre-, post-, and delayed

Scoring is simple and fast for a third-grade classroom: one point per correctly matched word, for a maximum of 10 points. The same 10 words used in the matching game and in the workbook unit are the ones tested, so the instrument measures exactly what both groups practised.

04 · instrument 2

Short Perception Questionnaire

two versions: one for the teacher, one (simplified) for students in Group A
Teacher — open question How practical was it to include the matching game in your regular class time and materials?
Teacher — open question What difficulties, if any, did you notice while using the game with this group?
Students — simple face-scale question Did you like playing with the picture-word cards? 🙁 😐 🙂
Students — simple face-scale question Was it easy to find the matching word for each picture? 🙁 😐 🙂

Keeping the student version to two quick face-scale questions matches the age of the participants and the limited class time available, while the teacher's open questions provide the richer detail needed to judge practicality.
