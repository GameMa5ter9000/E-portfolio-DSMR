Oral Presentation Support: Two Applied Data Collection Instruments

This page gathers, in one place, the two instruments selected for the Week 11 oral presentation — the design method behind them, how and when each was applied, and the evidence to bring to the session.

01 · design method(s) and authors

Overall Design

This study follows a quasi-experimental, nonequivalent comparison group design (Burke & Christensen, 2014), comparing two intact third-grade groups — matching game versus workbook practice — without random assignment, supported by a pretest to check initial group equivalence. Its quantitative core is complemented with qualitative tools inspired by observational and ethnographic practice (classroom field notes and a short focus group), in line with the mixed, low-cost approach justified since Entry #1.

02 · the two instruments selected for this presentation

Instruments Applied

1. Vocabulary Test

The 10-item picture-to-word test (Entry #2), applied pre-, post-, and two weeks later to both groups. Chosen because it directly answers the research question: whether the matching game improves immediate learning and retention compared with workbook practice.

Designed in Entry #2 · applied per Entry #6's design

2. Classroom Field Notes

The observation template (Entry #3), completed once per session for each group. Chosen because it captures how the activity actually unfolded — attention, participation, use of the target words — something the test scores alone cannot show.

Designed in Entry #3
