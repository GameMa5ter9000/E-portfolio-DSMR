Using a Picture-Word Matching Game to Improve Classroom Vocabulary Learning in Colombian Third Graders

A practical, low-cost classroom intervention: pairing pictures with words to help third graders in a Colombian public primary school learn — and actually remember — everyday classroom-object vocabulary in English.

02 · updated research question

Research Question

To what extent does an in-class picture–word matching game improve immediate learning and two-week retention of classroom-object vocabulary among Grade-3 students in a Colombian public primary school, compared with workbook-based practice?

Activity: matching game Focus: classroom-object vocabulary Group: 3rd graders Outcomes: immediate + 2-week retention
03 · general & specific objectives

Objectives

General objective

To evaluate the extent to which an in-class picture–word matching game improves immediate learning and two-week retention of classroom-object vocabulary among Grade-3 students in a Colombian public primary school, compared with workbook-based practice.

  • 1

    Compare the immediate vocabulary test scores of students who practise with the picture–word matching game and students who practise with workbook activities.

  • 2

    Compare the delayed vocabulary test scores of the two groups, in order to examine differences in vocabulary retention.

  • 3

    Design and implement a low-cost, picture–word matching game that can be integrated into regular English lessons in a third-grade public school class.

  • 4

    Describe the teacher's and students' perceptions of the practicality and ease of use of the matching game in this specific classroom context.

04 · why this research matters

Summary of the Justification

Educational & social

Stronger English vocabulary in the early grades widens future opportunities in study, work, and communication — and helps children feel English belongs to their daily life, not just the textbook.

Pedagogical

A picture-word matching game is a concrete, low-cost tool built from materials teachers already have — printed cards and class time — that can be reused across other vocabulary units.

Research & feasibility

Few studies test a single, non-digital game under real Colombian public-school conditions. Simple materials and short tests make this project realistic for the researcher and the school alike.
